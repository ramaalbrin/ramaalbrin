Components of a Project Feasibility Study: Marketing, Technical & Financial Analysis

What are the Core Elements of a Project Feasibility Study?

A comprehensive project feasibility study goes far beyond a simple budget. It dissects every major aspect of the enterprise to ensure smooth operation when the project launches.

1. Marketing Feasibility

Considered the most important stage, it focuses on marketing arrangements for expected outputs and evaluating the supply of inputs. Without careful market analysis, the project will not succeed no matter how innovative.

The expert must determine the place of sale, size and breadth of the market, and set creative solutions including the Four Ps: Product, Price, Promotion, and Place.

2. Technical Feasibility

A fundamental pillar upon which subsequent financial and economic studies are based. It decides the technical validity of the project, determines the site, machinery, and production line planning.

3. Financial Feasibility

Formed from marketing and technical outputs to know costs and revenues. Costs are divided primarily into:

  • Investment (Capital) Costs: Everything spent from the idea's inception to the first production cycle (like buildings/machines).
  • Operating Costs: Short-term costs for one operational cycle like wages, materials, and fuel.

4. Economic Feasibility

Similar to financial evaluation but with a larger societal economic orientation. It uses "shadow prices" to reflect the true economic value within the state's economy.

5. Social and Environmental Feasibility

Social feasibility concerns income distribution justice and job creation. Environmental feasibility focuses on the project's impact on environment and public health.

Sensitivity Analysis: Measures the project's resilience to factor changes (like material cost increase or sales drop).

Conclusion

Moving forward in startups carries great risks. Auditing marketing, technical, and financial feasibility is the shield and greatest guarantee for your investment's success.
